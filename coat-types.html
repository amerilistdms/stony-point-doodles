Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle coat types

What to know about wavy, curly, and straight coats before choosing your puppy.

Before you choose

More than the fluffiest face

One of the first things families notice about a Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle is the coat. Some puppies have soft, loose waves, others develop beautiful curls, and some have a straighter coat that shows more of their Bernese Mountain Dog heritage. Each look has its own charm, but coat type also affects grooming, shedding, and what you can expect as your puppy grows.

At Stony Point Doodles, we are a small, boutique, in-home Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle breeder in Rockland County, New York, serving families throughout the New York and New Jersey metro area. We believe choosing a puppy should be about finding the right companion for your family, not simply choosing a color or the fluffiest face.

Overview

What types of coats can Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodles have?

Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodles generally have three coat textures: straight, wavy, and curly. These are broad descriptions, and individual dogs can fall somewhere between them. A puppy’s adult coat may also look different from its soft puppy coat.

Wavy

The classic teddy bear look

The wavy coat is what many people picture when they think of a Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle. It has soft, loose waves and a relaxed, fluffy appearance that often gives the dog that adorable teddy bear look.

Wavy coats are sometimes called fleece coats. They may release less visible hair than straighter coats, but shedding varies from dog to dog. They also require regular brushing because loose hair and tangles can become trapped in the coat.

What to expect: A soft, shaggy appearance, regular brushing, and professional grooming to keep the coat comfortable and manageable.

Curly

More of the Poodle look

Curly Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodles have tighter, more defined curls that may resemble the Poodle side of their heritage. Some curls are loose and springy, while others are denser and more tightly formed.

Curly coats often release less visible hair around the home, which is one reason many families are interested in them. However, less visible shedding does not mean less grooming. Curly coats can trap loose hair and develop mats if they are not brushed and combed regularly.

What to expect: A curlier appearance, often less visible shedding, and a greater commitment to brushing, combing, and professional grooming.

Straight

A more Bernese-inspired appearance

A straight-coated Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle has less curl and may have a smoother, more relaxed appearance. Some straight coats still have a little wave, especially as the hair grows longer.

Straighter coats are often more likely to shed visibly, although coat genetics and individual variation matter. They may be easier to detangle than a dense curly coat, but they still need regular grooming and coat care.

What to expect: A more relaxed coat texture, potentially more visible shedding, and regular brushing to keep the coat healthy.

Compare

Coat types at a glance

Coat type Appearance Shedding Grooming
Wavy Soft, loose waves and a teddy bear look Often lower, but varies Regular brushing and professional grooming
Curly Tighter, more defined curls Often less visible shedding Frequent brushing and careful mat prevention
Straight Flatter, smoother coat with less curl Often more noticeable Regular brushing and coat maintenance

These are general tendencies, not guarantees. Two puppies from the same litter can have different coats and grooming needs.

Face & features

What are furnishings?

You may hear breeders use the term furnishings when discussing Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle coats. Furnishings refer to the longer facial hair that creates the familiar Doodle beard, eyebrows, and shaggy face.

A furnished Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle may have a straight, wavy, or curly body coat. An unfurnished Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle generally has a smoother face with less of the traditional Doodle beard.

Furnishings and curl are separate coat traits. That means a straight-coated Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle can still have the classic furnished Doodle face.

Hair around the home

Do Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodles shed?

Yes, Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodles can shed. The amount varies depending on the individual dog and its coat genetics.

Curly coats often release less visible hair, while straighter coats may shed more noticeably. Wavy coats can fall somewhere in between. However, coat texture alone cannot predict exactly how much a particular puppy will shed.

If shedding is an important consideration for your family, ask about the parents’ coats and any available coat genetic testing. A responsible breeder should be able to explain what is known and what cannot be guaranteed.

Allergies

Are they hypoallergenic?

No dog is completely hypoallergenic, including Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodles. Some people with allergies may tolerate certain dogs better than others, but there is no guarantee that a particular coat type will prevent an allergic reaction.

If someone in your household has dog allergies, we encourage you to spend time around dogs before making a commitment and speak with your healthcare professional if needed.

Choosing a puppy based on a promise of “100% hypoallergenic” is not something we recommend.

Growing up

Will my puppy’s coat change?

It can. A Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle puppy’s coat may become curlier, straighter, thicker, or different in texture as the adult coat develops. The soft puppy coat is not always a perfect preview of the finished adult coat.

This is one reason we encourage families to keep an open mind when selecting a puppy. We can discuss the parents’ coats and what we observe as the puppies grow, but we cannot promise an exact adult coat.

Care

How much grooming do they need?

Every Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle needs regular coat care. The amount of brushing will depend on the coat’s length, density, texture, and how easily it tangles.

A good routine includes brushing and combing, checking areas that commonly mat, and scheduling professional grooming as needed. Areas behind the ears, under the collar or harness, around the legs, and near the armpits deserve extra attention.

Many families find that a shorter, well-maintained haircut is easier to manage than a long coat. Your groomer can help you choose a style that works for your dog and your lifestyle.

Choosing well

Which coat type is best for your family?

There is no single best Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle coat. The right choice depends on what matters most to you.

If you love the classic teddy bear appearance, you may be drawn to a wavy coat. If less visible shedding is a priority and you are prepared for frequent grooming, a curly coat may appeal to you. If you prefer a more relaxed, Bernese-inspired appearance and do not mind more visible hair, a straighter coat may be a wonderful fit.

Most importantly, remember that temperament, health, and the right family match matter more than coat type alone.

Location

Choosing a puppy in New York or New Jersey

If you are searching for Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle puppies in New York, New Jersey, or the surrounding metro area, we encourage you to ask breeders about more than coat color and appearance.

Learn about the parents, health testing, how the puppies are raised, and what support is available after your puppy goes home. Ask about grooming expectations and whether the breeder can explain the coat traits of the litter you are considering.

At Stony Point Doodles, our puppies are raised in our home in Rockland County, NY. We enjoy getting to know our puppy families and helping them understand the personalities and characteristics of the puppies as they grow.

We are happy to discuss coat preferences, but we believe the best match is one that considers the whole puppy.
