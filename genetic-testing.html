Can Two Genetically Healthy Dogs Produce Puppies With Health Problems?

The truth about genetic testing.

By Stony Point Doodles

A valuable tool, not a crystal ball

When searching for the perfect puppy, one of the most important questions families ask is whether the parents have been genetically tested.

And it’s a great question!

At Stony Point Doodles, we believe every family deserves to understand where their puppy comes from, the health of its parents, and what responsible breeding really means.

But there’s one common misconception we’d like to clear up.

Just because two dogs have excellent genetic testing results does not mean their puppies are guaranteed to be perfectly healthy.

Genetic testing is an incredibly valuable tool, but it’s not a crystal ball. Just like with human children, even two healthy parents can have offspring who develop unexpected health conditions.

Let’s explore why.

The science

What is genetic testing in dogs?

Modern veterinary science has made remarkable advances in canine genetic testing.

Today, breeders can screen dogs for many known inherited conditions, helping them make more informed breeding decisions and reduce the risk of passing certain diseases to future generations.

Depending on the breeds involved, these tests may identify genetic variants associated with conditions such as:

  • Progressive Retinal Atrophy (PRA), which can lead to vision loss
  • Degenerative Myelopathy (DM), a neurological disease
  • Von Willebrand Disease, an inherited bleeding disorder
  • Certain inherited metabolic and neurological conditions

These screenings are important because they allow breeders to identify specific genetic risks before pairing two dogs.

However, genetic testing only evaluates the conditions and genetic variants included in the test.

A dog testing clear for 200 genetic conditions does not mean that dog is free from every possible hereditary or medical condition.

The honest answer

Can two genetically clear parents still produce a puppy with health problems?

Yes, absolutely.

This is something every prospective puppy owner should understand.

When two dogs test clear for a particular disease causing variant, their puppies generally will not inherit that specific variant, assuming the results and parentage are accurate.

That’s wonderful news!

But there are thousands of genes involved in a dog’s development, and scientists have not identified every genetic factor responsible for every canine health condition.

Some diseases involve multiple genes working together. Others are influenced by environmental factors, nutrition, growth, injury, or simply biological circumstances that cannot always be predicted.

For example, a puppy could develop hip dysplasia even when both parents have favorable hip evaluations.

Similarly, a puppy might experience allergies, orthopedic problems, digestive sensitivities, or other medical issues despite coming from carefully screened parents.

This doesn’t automatically mean the breeder did something wrong or that the parents were unhealthy.

It means biology is complicated.

A fuller picture

Why DNA testing alone is not enough

One of the biggest misunderstandings about responsible breeding is the belief that a DNA test covers everything.

It doesn’t.

Some health conditions, including hip and elbow dysplasia, require additional veterinary screening and evaluation.

Organizations such as the Orthopedic Foundation for Animals (OFA) help breeders evaluate inherited health risks through various screening programs.

Depending on the breeds involved, responsible health evaluations may include:

  • Hip and elbow evaluations
  • Eye examinations
  • Cardiac screening
  • Appropriate DNA testing for inherited diseases
  • Review of family health history and previous offspring

These evaluations provide a more complete picture of a breeding dog’s health.

For Bernedoodles, it’s particularly important to consider health concerns associated with both Bernese Mountain Dogs and Poodles.

No single DNA panel replaces the need for appropriate veterinary examinations and responsible breeding decisions.

The whole picture

Genetics are only part of the equation

Think about human families for a moment.

Two healthy parents can have children who develop allergies, medical conditions, orthopedic problems, or other health challenges.

Dogs are no different.

Genetics influence health, but so do many other factors.

A puppy’s nutrition, growth rate, body weight, exercise, environment, and veterinary care all contribute to its overall health and development.

Accidents and injuries can also happen, regardless of how carefully a puppy was bred.

Even the most responsible breeder cannot eliminate every possibility of illness or injury throughout a dog’s life.

What breeders can do is take reasonable, scientifically informed steps to reduce known risks and provide puppies with the healthiest possible start.

Honesty first

What does responsible breeding really mean?

At Stony Point Doodles, we believe responsible breeding is about much more than producing beautiful puppies.

It’s about understanding the responsibility that comes with bringing new lives into the world.

A thoughtful breeding program should prioritize the health and well being of the parent dogs, appropriate health screening, temperament, genetic compatibility, and the environment in which puppies are raised.

It also means being honest with prospective families.

We believe breeders should never promise something that science cannot guarantee.

Statements such as “Our puppies will never have genetic problems” or “Our puppies are guaranteed to be perfectly healthy because their parents were DNA tested” can create unrealistic expectations.

Even the most carefully planned breeding carries some degree of uncertainty.

Responsible breeding is about reducing risks, not pretending those risks don’t exist.

Raised at home

Why the first eight weeks matter so much

Genetics help shape a puppy, but the environment in which that puppy is raised also plays an important role.

The first several weeks of life are a critical developmental period.

Proper nutrition, cleanliness, veterinary attention, gentle handling, early socialization, and positive exposure to everyday household experiences all contribute to a puppy’s development.

This is one of the reasons we love raising our puppies in a home environment.

We want our puppies to experience the sights, sounds, affection, and everyday routines of family life before joining their forever homes.

We believe that raising puppies with love, patience, and attention helps prepare them for a successful transition into their new families.

Peace of mind

What about health guarantees?

Many responsible breeders offer written health guarantees covering certain serious congenital or hereditary conditions.

These guarantees can provide families with valuable protection and peace of mind.

However, it’s important to understand that a health guarantee is not a promise that a puppy will never become sick.

Rather, it is a written commitment outlining what the breeder will do if a qualifying health condition develops within a specified period.

Families should always review the terms of any health guarantee carefully and understand what is covered.

At Stony Point Doodles, we believe transparency and clear communication are essential to building trust with our puppy families.

How we work

Our philosophy at Stony Point Doodles

We love our dogs, and we love seeing the happiness they bring to the families who welcome them into their homes.

We also recognize that breeding comes with tremendous responsibility.

Our goal is not simply to produce adorable Bernedoodle puppies. It’s to make thoughtful decisions, prioritize the well being of our dogs, provide a loving environment, and help families understand the lifelong commitment of dog ownership.

We believe in continuing to learn, asking questions, listening to veterinary professionals, and making decisions based on the best information available.

Most importantly, we believe in being honest.

No breeder can promise a perfect puppy.

No genetic test can predict every possible health concern.

And no amount of preparation can eliminate every uncertainty that comes with raising a living, growing animal.

But responsible breeding practices, appropriate health screening, attentive care, and informed puppy families can make a meaningful difference.

Ask questions

Healthy parents are a great start, not a guarantee

If you’re researching breeders and preparing to welcome a puppy into your family, we encourage you to ask questions.

Ask about the parents. Ask about health testing. Ask about veterinary care, temperament, socialization, and how the puppies are raised.

And be cautious of anyone who promises that their puppies will never experience health problems simply because the parents have been genetically tested.

The goal of responsible breeding isn’t to promise perfection. It’s to give every puppy the best possible opportunity for a healthy, happy, and fulfilling life.

At Stony Point Doodles, that’s a philosophy we believe every puppy deserves.
