Keeping Your Bernedoodle Looking and Feeling Their Best

A practical grooming guide for Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle families.

Start here

A little grooming goes a long way

That beautiful Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle coat comes with responsibility. Regular grooming is part of life with a Doodle, whether your dog has a wavy coat, a curly coat, or something in between. The earlier you start, the easier it becomes for both of you.

Grooming is not just about appearance. It is a chance to check your dog's skin, ears, paws, and overall comfort while building trust. Short, positive sessions from puppyhood make a real difference as your dog grows.

Daily care

Brushing and combing

Mats can form quickly on a Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle, especially in areas where the coat rubs or folds. Frequent brushing helps prevent tangles before they become painful.

Pay extra attention to these spots:

  • Behind and inside the ears
  • Under the collar or harness
  • Under the front legs and chest
  • The belly
  • Legs, paws, and tail
  • Where the legs meet the body

Brushing only the outer layer is not enough. Use a metal comb or butter comb and work from the skin outward to the ends of the hair. This helps you find hidden tangles before they tighten.

Puppyhood

Start grooming practice now

Do not wait until your puppy needs a full groom. Begin handling paws, toes, ears, face, muzzle, legs, and tail in short, calm sessions.

Introduce the brush gradually. Pair each session with treats and praise. Keep sessions short and positive. The goal is for your puppy to accept normal handling as a regular part of life, not something to dread.

Puppies who learn this early are usually much easier to groom as adults.

Professional care

The first haircut

Many groomers offer introductory puppy appointments designed to get young dogs comfortable with the salon environment. These visits may include a bath, light trim, nail check, and time on the table without a full haircut.

Your puppy's first several months at home are a good time to build positive associations with grooming. Talk with your groomer about what makes sense for your puppy's age and coat.

Schedule

Professional grooming frequency

Every 6 to 8 weeks is a common starting point for professional grooming, but the right schedule depends on your dog's coat type, length, growth rate, brushing routine, activity level, and preferred style.

Shorter coats may need less frequent visits but still require regular brushing at home. Longer coats often need more frequent professional care and daily attention between appointments.

Your groomer can help you find a maintenance plan that fits your dog and your lifestyle.

At home

Bath time

Use a gentle dog shampoo formulated for canine skin and coat. At Stony Point Doodles, we use Cowboy Magic Rosewater Pet Shampoo and Conditioner on our dogs.

Rinse thoroughly. Leftover shampoo can irritate skin and make the coat feel rough or greasy.

Brush before bathing whenever possible. Wet mats are harder to remove. After the bath, dry your dog fully and brush again once the coat is dry.

Health check

Ears

Floppy ears can trap moisture and debris. During grooming, check for redness, odor, excess wax, or discharge.

Ask your veterinarian or groomer to show you safe ear cleaning if you are unsure how to do it. Contact your vet if you notice persistent odor, scratching, head shaking, or discharge.

Hands on

Paws and nails

Trim nails regularly. Long nails can affect posture and comfort. Handle paws from a young age so nail trims feel normal rather than scary.

If you are not comfortable trimming nails yourself, your groomer or veterinarian can help. Also check for hair between the paw pads, which can collect debris and cause slipping or irritation.

Dental care

Teeth

Use a dog toothbrush and dog toothpaste only. Never use human toothpaste, which can be harmful to dogs.

Your veterinarian can recommend a dental care routine based on your dog's age and needs. Even a few minutes of brushing several times a week can make a difference over time.

Warm weather

Summer haircuts

Many families choose a shorter trim during warm weather or during active outdoor seasons. A shorter coat can be easier to manage, but it still needs regular brushing and professional maintenance.

Talk with your groomer about a length that keeps your dog comfortable without compromising coat health.

Why it matters

Why preventing mats matters

Mats are not just a cosmetic issue. They can pull on the skin, trap moisture, hide skin problems, and become painful.

Severe matting may require a close clip to remove safely. That is not something any groomer wants to do unnecessarily, and it is not fair to blame the groomer when matting could have been prevented at home.

Regular brushing is the best way to keep your Multigenerational Mini‑Bernedoodle comfortable and your grooming appointments straightforward.

Together

Make grooming bonding time

Put on calm music, offer treats, and use plenty of praise. Grooming can become something your dog tolerates, or even enjoys, when it is approached with patience and consistency.

Adult dogs are usually easier to groom when good habits start in puppyhood. A steady routine keeps your Stony Point Doodle comfortable, healthy, and looking their best.
