The breed

What is a Mini‑Bernedoodle?

A Mini‑Bernedoodle is a smaller-size cross between a Bernese Mountain Dog and a Poodle. The combination is often associated with the affectionate nature of the Bernese Mountain Dog and the intelligence and trainability of the Poodle.

Mini‑Bernedoodles are mixed breed dogs, which means their appearance, coat, size, and personality can vary. Even puppies from the same litter may develop different characteristics. That individuality is part of what makes getting to know each puppy so important.

Personality

What is a Mini‑Bernedoodle’s temperament like?

Mini‑Bernedoodles are often described as loving, playful, intelligent, and family oriented. Many enjoy being close to their people, whether that means joining a family activity, playing outside, or relaxing at home.

However, no puppy comes with a guaranteed personality. Some may be more outgoing, while others are quieter or more independent. Early socialization, consistent training, and the individual puppy’s temperament all play an important role in the dog they become.

For families with children, a Mini‑Bernedoodle can be a wonderful companion, but children should always be taught how to interact respectfully with dogs. Supervision is especially important with young puppies.

Growth

How big do Mini‑Bernedoodles get?

Mini‑Bernedoodles are bred to stay more compact than a standard Bernedoodle. Even so, adult size still depends on the parents, genetics, and individual development.

When considering a puppy, ask the breeder about the size of both parents and the expected adult size of the litter. Keep in mind that any estimate is just that: an estimate. A puppy may grow larger or smaller than expected.

At Stony Point Doodles, we are happy to discuss the parents of each litter and help families understand what they may reasonably expect as their puppy grows.

Coat & allergies

Do Mini‑Bernedoodles shed? Are they hypoallergenic?

One of the most common questions about Mini‑Bernedoodles is whether they shed. Many Mini‑Bernedoodles have coats that shed less than some other dogs, but shedding varies depending on the individual dog and its coat genetics.

No dog is completely hypoallergenic. People with allergies may react to proteins found in a dog’s saliva, skin, and other sources, not simply to loose hair. If allergies are a concern, it is important to spend time around dogs and speak with your healthcare professional before making a commitment.

A low shedding coat also does not mean a low maintenance coat. In fact, many Mini‑Bernedoodles require substantial grooming.

Care

How much grooming does a Mini‑Bernedoodle need?

Mini‑Bernedoodles can have straight, wavy, or curly coats. Their coat type affects how much brushing and grooming they need, but all owners should be prepared for regular maintenance.

Brushing helps prevent tangles and matting, especially around the ears, legs, collar area, and other places where friction occurs. Many Mini‑Bernedoodles also benefit from regular appointments with a professional groomer.

Before bringing your puppy home, consider whether you have the time and budget for ongoing coat care. Grooming is an important part of keeping your dog comfortable and healthy.

A Mini‑Bernedoodle is not just a beautiful dog. It is a long-term commitment and a new member of your family.

Energy

How much exercise does a Mini‑Bernedoodle need?

Mini‑Bernedoodles generally enjoy a combination of physical activity and mental stimulation. Walks, playtime, training games, and opportunities to explore can all help keep them engaged.

Their exercise needs will vary by age, size, and personality. A young puppy should not be expected to keep up with an adult dog, and exercise should be appropriate for their stage of development.

A Mini‑Bernedoodle does not necessarily need a huge property, but they do need a family that can provide attention, activity, and a consistent routine.

Learning

Are Mini‑Bernedoodles easy to train?

Mini‑Bernedoodles are often intelligent and responsive to training, but that does not mean they train themselves. Like any puppy, they need patience, consistency, and clear expectations.

Positive reinforcement training can help teach important skills such as potty training, crate training, walking on a leash, and responding to basic cues. Short, enjoyable training sessions are especially helpful for young puppies.

Starting early and making training part of everyday life can help your Mini‑Bernedoodle grow into a well-mannered family companion.

Health

What health considerations should you know about?

As a cross between a Bernese Mountain Dog and a Poodle, a Mini‑Bernedoodle may inherit health risks associated with either parent breed. Responsible breeding practices can help reduce certain risks, but no breeder can guarantee that a puppy will never develop a health condition.

When researching breeders, ask about the parents’ health history, genetic testing, and veterinary screening. DNA testing is valuable, but it does not replace every type of health evaluation. Depending on the parent breeds and individual dogs, screening may include orthopedic, eye, cardiac, and other recommended evaluations.

At Stony Point Doodles, our breeding dogs receive regular veterinary care and have undergone DNA testing. We are happy to discuss the health information available for the parents of the litter you are considering.

Finding the right fit

What should you look for in a Mini‑Bernedoodle breeder?

Finding the right breeder is just as important as choosing the right puppy. A responsible breeder should be willing to answer your questions and help you understand how their puppies are raised.

Consider asking where the puppies live, whether you can meet the parents, what health testing has been completed, how the puppies are socialized, and what support is available after they go home. You should also review the purchase agreement and health guarantee before placing a deposit.

A good breeder should care about where their puppies are going, not simply how quickly they can sell them.

Our approach

Next steps

Before making your decision, think about your daily schedule, living space, budget, and the amount of time you can devote to a puppy.
